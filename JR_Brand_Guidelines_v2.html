Section 01

Visual direction

The brand feels like a high-end design studio that also understands operations. Strategic, not decorative. Editorial, not loud. Every visual decision is justified.

Aesthetic reference
Notion × Linear × Framer
— but with a human story behind it.
Not a startup template. Not a portfolio cliché. A personal brand with a point of view.
Personality words
Strategic Editorial Minimal Premium Structured Intelligent Slightly bold Operator-minded
Layout style
Grid-disciplined. Generous whitespace. Left-aligned by default. Asymmetry only when intentional. Structure is visible but not rigid.
Composition rule
One dominant element per frame. Headlines carry weight — everything else supports. Never compete with your headline.
Tone
Editorial with precision. Think: systems thinking meets a design magazine. Clear hierarchy. No decoration without function.
Section 01b

Brand voice

How the brand sounds is as important as how it looks. These rules apply to captions, headlines, case study copy, and social posts.

Writing style
Direct. Declarative. No filler words. Lead with the insight, not the context. Short sentences. Numbers over adjectives.

Write: "423% reservation growth. Built from scratch."
Not: "I'm incredibly passionate about helping businesses grow through innovative design solutions."
Headline rules
— Start with the outcome or the tension
— Use sentence case (not Title Case)
— Maximum 8 words for social headlines
— Never use exclamation marks
— Numbers always as numerals: 10, not ten
— Em dash for emphasis, not parentheses
Voice: what JR sounds like
The operator who also designs. Confident without being loud. Says what others only think. Backs opinions with evidence. Never hedges.
Voice: what JR never sounds like
Hustle culture. Inspirational poster. "My journey." Buzzword stacking. Humble bragging. Vague thought leadership. Excessive emoji.
Section 02

Color system

Four colors. Each with a specific job. The system works because of restraint — not variety.

Dark base
#202020
Primary background. All dark-mode surfaces. Default canvas.
Light neutral
#e4e2dd
Primary text on dark. Light-mode canvas. Off-white — warmer than pure white.
Primary accent
#0053e1
CTAs, links, data highlights, eyebrows, active states. The intelligence signal.
Emphasis red
#b60d0d
Sparingly. Contrarian moments, critical callouts, tension statements. Max 1 use per layout.
The restraint rule: Blue appears on 20–30% of elements max. Red appears on 1 element per layout maximum. Most of your visual field should be dark + light neutral contrast. Color earns its place.
Dark mode rules (default)
— Canvas: #202020
— Primary text: #e4e2dd
— Muted text: rgba(32,32,32,0.45)
— Borders: rgba(32,32,32,0.1)
— Surface lift: rgba(32,32,32,0.03)
— Accent: #0053e1 — max 30% of frame
— Red: #b60d0d — max 1 use per layout
Light mode rules (case studies / landing pages)
— Canvas: #e4e2dd
— Primary text: #202020
— Muted text: rgba(32,32,32,0.45)
— Borders: rgba(32,32,32,0.12)
— Accent: #0053e1 — same rule
— Dark blocks: #202020 sections for contrast
— Never use pure white (#ffffff) — always #e4e2dd
Color use by context
Social content
Dark canvas, light text. Blue for eyebrows and stats only. Red never appears on Instagram/Threads — too aggressive at small sizes.
Case studies
Light canvas preferred. Dark section blocks for contrast. Blue for stat callouts, section labels, data highlights. Red for critical findings only.
Dashboards / Airtable
Follow the platform. Use blue for primary actions and active states. Use red only for alert/error states. Never override platform chrome with brand colors.

Section 05

Layout system

Consistent grids across all formats. Left-aligned by default. Centered only for hero moments or deliberate editorial emphasis.

Grid — social (1080×1080)
— 48px margin all sides
— 12-column grid, 16px gutter
— Content in columns 1–8 (left) or 3–10 (centered)
— Always align headline to left edge
— Eyebrow label: top-left
— Logo/handle: bottom-left
— Stat or accent: bottom-right
Grid — carousel (1080×1350)
— 56px left/right margin
— Slide 1: cover — full bleed headline, eyebrow, handle
— Slides 2–N: consistent left margin, section label top, content center
— Last slide: CTA + handle + link
— Keep 1 idea per slide. Never 2.
Grid — landing page (desktop)
— Max content width: 1200px
— Page margins: 96px left/right
— 12-column grid, 24px gutter
— Hero text: columns 1–7
— Body text max-width: 600px
— Section padding: 96px top/bottom
Grid — case study page
— Header/meta: full width
— Body copy: max 680px, centered
— Stat callouts: full width, dark background
— Images: full bleed or max 880px
— Tool/process tables: full width
— Section dividers: 1px border or full-width spacing
Alignment rules
Left-aligned: 90% of all content. Centered: hero headlines on mobile only, pull quotes, stat moments. Right-aligned: never, except metadata or timestamps.
Border / divider rules
Use 1px borders at 12% opacity for structure. Never use drop shadows — borders are the JR way. Dividers are horizontal only. No decorative rules or ornaments.
Imagery rules
Screenshots: always in device frame or with a border. No floating pngs. Photos: desaturated or with a dark overlay. Never stock photography of people. Real screenshots > mockups.

Section 07

Motion & visual accents

Motion is not decoration. It proves capability. Every screen recording or loop should show something real — a system working, a process flowing, a decision being made.

Screen recordings — what works
— Airtable views switching between Grid / Gallery / Dashboard
— Figma: auto-layout adjusting, component variants switching
— Guesty/Operto: booking flow, message automation
— Notion: linked databases, formula fields
— Keep to 6–15 seconds. No audio. No cursor fumbling.
— Always record at 2x or retina. Never upscale.
GIF / loop rules
— Max 3 seconds per loop
— One thing happening per loop — not a full workflow demo
— No text animations (no fly-in, no typewriter effect)
— Subtle movement: a chart filling, a status changing, a view filtering
— Always place in a device frame or bordered container
— Never autoplay with sound in any context
What motion proves
You built real things. A 6-second Airtable screen recording is more convincing than 3 paragraphs of copy. Use motion where static screenshots leave questions unanswered.
What to avoid
— Transitions for their own sake
— Lottie animations as decoration
— Slide-in text on social posts
— Motion that plays before content loads
— Anything that loops more than 3x without pause
Premium motion feel
— Ease-out curves (never linear)
— Duration: 200–400ms for UI, 800–1200ms for reveals
— If something moves, only one thing moves at a time
— Delay between elements: 80–120ms stagger
— When in doubt: don't animate
Section 08

Do / Don't

These are constraints, not suggestions. They protect the system from drift.

DO
  • Use real numbers whenever possible — 423%, $609K, 10 years
  • Lead with outcomes, not process
  • Use Garet at display scale for all hero moments and section headlines
  • Keep social layouts left-aligned with generous margins
  • Show real screenshots — Airtable, Figma, Operto, Guesty
  • Use blue sparingly — max 30% of any frame
  • Keep 1 idea per slide, 1 dominant element per layout
  • Use mono-spaced type for labels, stats, and eyebrows
  • Put your handle on every piece of content
  • Use sentence case for all headlines
  • Respect 8px spacing increments in Figma
  • Let whitespace do the heavy lifting
  • Use borders instead of shadows for structural separation
DON'T
  • Use Horizon or Neue Einstellung — they're retired from the system
  • Use pure white (#ffffff) — always use #e4e2dd
  • Use red more than once per layout
  • Use drop shadows — ever
  • Center-align body text
  • Use gradients — flat color only
  • Mix more than 2 typefaces in a single layout
  • Use decorative dividers or ornaments
  • Animate text on social posts (no typewriter, no fly-in)
  • Use stock photography of people
  • Write "I'm passionate about..." anywhere
  • Use emoji in professional contexts (case studies, landing pages)
  • Post without your handle visible
  • Use Title Case for headlines
  • Compress images — always export at full resolution
